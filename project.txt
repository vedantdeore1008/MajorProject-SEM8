AI-Powered Viva Interview System — Complete Technical Document 1. One-line elevator pitch An intelligent, fully-automated oral examination system that uses voice cloning, AI-generated questions grounded in curriculum context, real-time speech-to-text transcription, and computer-vision-based proctoring to deliver authentic, scalable, and integrity-verified viva experiences. 2. 30–45 second verbal opener I built an AI-powered viva interview system that transforms traditional oral exams into scalable, automated experiences while maintaining academic rigor and integrity. Teachers upload a short audio sample (5 minutes), and our system creates a realistic voice clone that asks AI-generated questions derived from their curriculum notes. During each viva session, students hear their teacher’s cloned voice asking context-grounded questions in real time. React Speech Recognition captures student responses and transcribes them live, while YOLOv8-based proctoring monitors the webcam to detect cheating behaviors like phones or proxy test-takers. The Flask AI microservice evaluates each response using a RAG pipeline—retrieving relevant context from teacher notes and scoring against a detailed rubric covering clarity, reasoning, evidence, and knowledge depth. The entire session—questions, answers, scores, feedback, and proctoring events—is stored in MongoDB with full transcripts and analytics. Teachers get comprehensive dashboards showing class viva performance, individual response quality, and proctoring violations. Students receive personalized feedback highlighting strengths and improvement areas, grounded in their teacher’s curriculum. Key interview talking points:- “We separated viva processing into a Flask microservice, enabling independent scaling without burdening the Node backend.”- “Voice cloning creates an authentic teacher-student interaction at scale— no more hiring human examiners.”- “RAG-grounded question generation ensures questions are always relevant to curriculum, eliminating hallucinated or off-topic queries.”- “YOLOv8 real-time proctoring flags violations without interrupting the student, giving teachers actionable evidence for post-viva review.”- “Full transcript storage enables students to review their viva and teachers to analyze response patterns across classes.” 13. Tech stack (with detailed architecture decisions) React Speech Recognition (Browser-native STT) Why chosen: React Speech Recognition wraps the browser’s native Web Speech API, enabling real-time, client-side speech-to-text transcription without sending audio to external servers. This eliminates latency, reduces infrastructure costs, and avoids exposing student voice data to third-party cloud services. The library provides event hooks (onResult, onError, onEnd) that integrate seamlessly with React state management, enabling live transcript display and error handling. Why NOT Google Cloud STT/Whisper:- Google Cloud STT: Requires network round-trips for every speech segment, introducing 500ms+ latency between student speaking and text appearing. Also incurs per-minute charges, scaling costs as viva volume grows.- Whisper (OpenAI): Requires server-side processing and model hosting (GPU intensive). Adds API calls, increases latency, and requires backend infrastructure to manage Whisper instances. Browser-native APIs avoid all this overhead. Decision: Browser-native speech recognition keeps the viva responsive and interactive—students see their words transcribed in real-time, confirming the system is listening. Latency perception matters for user experience in live exams. LMNT TTS (Voice Cloning for Teacher Questions) Whychosen: LMNTTTSprovideshigh-fidelity voice cloning from short audio samples (5 minutes minimum). Teachers record themselves reading a passage, and LMNT’s neural models learn speech patterns (tone, pace, accent) to synthesize new questions in the teacher’s voice. The API is stable, offers sub-second latency (<300ms), and handles educational content with natural inflection and pauses. Why NOT ElevenLabs/Azure TTS/Google Wavenet:- ElevenLabs: Excellent quality but higher per-second costs ($0.30–$0.50/1K characters vs. LMNT’s $0.01–$0.02). Stricter commercial licensing terms for educational use. Overkill if LMNT quality suffices.- Azure TTS: Voice profiles exist but require manual tuning and don’t capture as much individual character. Less natural-sounding for viva contexts where authenticity matters.- Google Wavenet: Premium pricing, doesn’t require as little audio for cloning, and API is geared toward batch processing rather than interactive viva use. Decision: LMNT balances quality, cost, speed, and minimal setup. For 1000 viva sessions yearly generating 5 questions each (5000 questions × 100 tokens avg = 500K tokens), LMNT costs ~$10/month. ElevenLabs would cost 10–20× more. 2YOLOv8 (Real-time Object Detection for Proctoring) Why chosen: YOLOv8 is a state-of-the-art real-time object detection model (v8 Nano fits on CPU, v8 Medium uses GPU) that accurately identifies phones, multiple faces, suspicious objects, and movements. Pre-trained weights are available for common objects. The model outputs bounding boxes with confidence scores, enabling precise logging of violations (e.g., “phone detected at 15:34 with 92% confidence near student’s left hand”). Why NOT OpenCV Haar Cascades/MediaPipe:- Haar Cascades: Legacy approach, unreliable at modern angles and lighting conditions. Struggles with partially visible objects. High false-negative rate (misses ~40% of violations).- MediaPipe: Designed for pose/hand tracking, not object detection. Doesn’t reliably detect phones, forbidden objects, or environmental anomalies.- Tensorflow Lite SSD: Slower inference on CPU, less accurate for edge detection. Decision: YOLOv8 provides modern accuracy (95%+ precision on standard objects) with efficient CPU inference (30FPS on i5 processor). Bounding boxes enable teachers to review violations with visual evidence, not just flags. Flask Microservice (AI Processing for Viva Evaluation) Whychosen: Viva evaluation involves multiple sequential operations (RAG retrieval, LLM scoring, response generation) that must complete within a studentperceived response time. Flask’s lightweight, synchronous model is ideal for batch operations. The microservice isolates heavy AI computation from the Node backend, preventing CPU/memory spikes from blocking API requests to other users. Flask integrates seamlessly with Python libraries for AI/ML (LangChain, ChromaDB, OpenCV for proctoring). Why NOT FastAPI/Django:- FastAPI: Async performance is overkill for batch viva evaluation. Our workloads are I/O-bound (waiting for LLM API responses), not compute-bound. FastAPI adds complexity without proportional benefit.- Django: Heavyweight ORM and admin panel unnecessary; viva evaluation is stateless. Django adds ~500ms startup time. Decision: Flask’s simplicity and Python ecosystem make it the optimal choice for a specialized AI evaluation microservice. Can migrate to FastAPI later if async throughput becomes a bottleneck. ChromaDB (RAG Vector Database for Question Context) Why chosen: When generating viva questions, AI models must ground questions in teacher-provided curriculum. ChromaDB stores teacher notes as vector 3embeddings with metadata (source file, page, chunk). During question generation, ChromaDB’s similarity search retrieves the top-K most relevant chunks, which are sent to the LLM as context—ensuring questions match curriculum depth and topics. Why NOTFAISS/Pinecone/Weaviate:- FAISS: Requires manual persistence layer and index serialization. No built-in metadata filtering.- Pinecone: Cloud-hosted, introduces 50–200ms latency per query. Subscription costs scale with index size.- Weaviate: More complex setup, requires Docker orchestration for production. Decision: ChromaDB’s embedded nature, simple Python API, and built-in metadata filtering align perfectly with our Flask microservice architecture. Runs locally, zero latency, zero subscription costs. Gemini 1.5 Flash LLM (Response Evaluation & Question Generation) Why chosen: Gemini 1.5 Flash is fast (2–5 second inference), cost-effective ($0.075 per 1M input tokens), and excels at structured tasks like rubric-based scoring and feedback generation. The 1M token context window means full transcripts + rubric + feedback fit in a single API call without chunking. Supports tool-use for returning structured JSON (scores, feedback categories). Why NOT GPT-4/Claude/Local LLMs:- GPT-4: 10–50× more expensive ($0.03–$0.06 per 1K tokens). Overkill reasoning power for viva evaluation; Flash suffices.- Claude 3 Opus: Similar cost concerns as GPT-4. Excellent reasoning but not necessary for rubric-based scoring.- Local LLMs (LLaMA, Mistral): Require GPU infrastructure ($300–$1000/month). Latency is 10–30 seconds per evaluation—too slow for interactive viva feedback loops. Decision: Gemini Flash balances speed, cost, and quality. For 5000 viva questions yearly with ~1000 tokens context each, cost is ~$375/year. Orders of magnitude cheaper than alternatives while maintaining quality. MongoDB (Viva Transcript & Metadata Storage) Why chosen: Viva reports contain hierarchical, variable-length data: questions (array), responses (array of text + timestamp), scores (nested rubric breakdown), proctoring events (time-series array), and feedback (structured per-response). MongoDB’s document model naturally represents this nested structure without schema migrations. Indexes on studentId, classroomId, vivaSessionId enable fast queries for dashboards. Why NOT PostgreSQL:- Schema migrations: Initial schema has 8 fields. After first viva session, requirements evolve—add plagiarism scores, add confidence ratings to proctoring events, add teacher comments. Each change requires 4migrations + downtime.- JSON handling: PostgreSQL’s JSON column is less efficient than MongoDB’s native document storage. Querying nested arrays is verbose (complex GROUP BY + JSONB operators). Decision: MongoDB’s flexible schema accelerates iteration during viva feature development. If strict schema enforcement becomes necessary (later maturity stages), can migrate to PostgreSQL then. Socket.io (Real-time Viva Session Updates) Why chosen: Viva sessions require bidirectional, low-latency communication: server sends next question and audio to client; client sends transcribed response back to server. Socket.io abstracts WebSocket fallbacks (polling, long-polling) and provides event-driven messaging, greatly simplifying real-time logic. Why NOT Native WebSockets/REST polling:- Native WebSockets: Requires manual connection management, reconnection logic, message serialization. 3–5× more boilerplate code.- REST polling: 2–5 second latency between events. Defeats purpose of interactive viva. Decision: Socket.io reduces complexity while providing production-grade reliability (auto-reconnect, event acknowledgment, room management for broadcasting to class dashboards). JWT Authentication (Stateless Session Management) Why chosen: Viva sessions span 20–60 minutes and involve multiple microservices (Node, Flask). JWT tokens enable stateless verification: token contains userId, role, sessionId. Both Node and Flask verify JWT signature without querying a session store. Scales horizontally—new server instances don’t need shared session cache. Why NOT Session Cookies/OAuth:- Session Cookies: Requires Redis/Memcached for distributed session storage. Adds dependency and complexity.- OAuth: Overkill for internal microservices. Adds latency for token introspection. Decision: JWTs provide lightweight, scalable authentication across Node + Flask boundary. Multer (PDF/Audio File Upload Middleware) Why chosen: Teachers upload curriculum notes (PDFs) and voice samples (MP3/WAV). Multer middleware handles multipart form-data parsing, saves 5f iles to disk, and provides file metadata (size, mimetype). Integrates seamlessly with Express routing. Decision: Multer is the standard Node.js solution for file uploads. No reason to reinvent. 4. User-visible features (Viva-specific) Teacher Voice Profile Setup Teachers record a 5-minute audio sample (reading curriculum passages). The system uploads to LMNT API, creates a voice clone, and stores the profile. Teachers can record multiple voice profiles (e.g., “casual explanation voice” vs. “formal exam voice”). The cloned voice is used exclusively for all viva questions in that classroom, creating consistent, authentic student experience. Viva Session Scheduling & Question Generation Teachers create viva sessions specifying topic, number of questions (5–10 typical), duration (20–60 minutes), and difficulty level. The system automatically generates topic-relevant questions using Gemini 1.5 Flash + RAG retrieval from teacher notes, ensuring questions match curriculum depth. Questions are stored and reviewed by the teacher (optional) before viva begins. Student Viva Interface with Live Audio Students start viva via a clean, distraction-free interface. Webcam and microphone are requested. The system plays the first teacher-cloned question via speaker. Students see the question text on screen and speak their answer. React Speech Recognition captures audio and displays a live transcript as the student speaks—visual feedback that the system is listening. Real-time Transcription Display As students speak, their words appear on screen in real-time. Students can edit the transcript if speech recognition misinterprets words. This UX ensures students aren’t surprised by incorrect transcription later and builds confidence in the system. 6Automatic Response Evaluation with Rubric-based Scoring Once student finishes answering (via explicit “next” button or 3-second silence), Flask evaluates the response using RAG-grounded context and Gemini LLM. Score (0–10) is shown instantly, along with key feedback points. Students see immediate feedback, reinforcing learning within the viva session. Full Session Transcript with Question-by-Question Breakdown After viva completes, students receive a full transcript showing every question, their response, the system’s score, and detailed feedback. Transcripts are downloadable as PDF or stored in the portal for future review. Teachers can also download and archive transcripts for records. YOLOv8-based Proctoring with Violation Logging During viva, webcam frames are analyzed in real-time. YOLOv8 detects phones, multiple faces, suspicious objects, and environmental anomalies. Each violation is logged with timestamp, type (e.g., “phone detected”), confidence score, and frame screenshot. Violations don’t stop the viva—teacher reviews afterward and decides whether to invalidate the session. Post-Viva Proctoring Review Dashboard Teachers access proctoring logs showing timeline of violations with visual evidence (frame screenshots). Teachers can mark violations as “legitimate” (student briefly held phone to check time) or “serious” (clear cheating attempt). Flags are stored for institutional compliance and student appeals. Teacher Viva Analytics Dashboard Teachers see aggregated class viva performance: average scores, distribution of scores, common areas of weakness (e.g., “25% of class struggled with photosynthesis questions”). Teachers can drill down to individual student performance— viva history, response quality, proctoring flags. Analytics inform targeted review sessions. 7Student Viva Performance History Students see all past viva sessions in their profile: dates, scores, feedback, trends (e.g., “Your photosynthesis score improved 15% this month”). Students can compare their performance against class average and review past transcripts to track learning progress. Plagiarism/AI-Generated Content Detection (Viva Responses) Every viva transcript is analyzed for AI-generated or plagiarized content using Eden AI detector. If a response is flagged as likely AI-generated (e.g., overly formal language, suspicious patterns), it’s marked in the proctoring report for teacher review. This complements computer-vision proctoring. Voice Quality Feedback for Teachers When teachers upload voice samples, the system analyzes audio quality (volume levels, background noise, clarity). If quality is poor, the teacher is prompted to re-record. This ensures voice clones sound natural and students can hear questions clearly. Accessibility Features • Transcription for hearing-impaired: Full real-time captions of both questions and student responses. • Text-to-Speech fallback: If speech recognition fails, students can type answers instead. • High-contrast UI: Color-blind friendly interface. 5. Performance & scaling considerations Throughput Targets • Concurrent vivas: 50–100 simultaneous sessions • Question generation latency: 5–10 seconds per question • Response evaluation latency: 3–5 seconds per response • YOLOv8 inference latency: <200ms per frame (1 FPS = acceptable) Optimization Strategies Question Caching • Pre-generate questions before viva starts (as shown in workflow). • Cache audio MP3s in /uploads/audio-cache/{vivaSessionId}/ to avoid re-synthesizing. ChromaDB Optimization • Index embeddings with metadata (classroom_id, topic) for fast filtering. • Batch embedding generation for teacher notes (offline, not on critical path). LLM Prompt Optimization • Use shorter, focused prompts to Gemini (reduce token usage, latency). • Cache system prompts (rubric definitions) to reduce redundant tokens. YOLOv8 Optimization • Use YOLOv8 Nano (faster, lighter) for real-time proctoring. • Resize frames to 640×480 before inference (standard YOLO input). • Throttle frame capture to 1 FPS (sufficient for violation detection). Network Optimization • Compress audio frames to JPEG quality 0.8 (reduces bandwidth by 50%). • Use Socket.io room management to broadcast aggregated proctoring events (not per-frame). Scaling Patterns • Horizontal scaling: Deploy multiple Flask instances behind load balancer; Node processes WebSocket events independently. • Async job queues: For batch processing (plagiarism detection for 100 viva transcripts), use background jobs (Celery + Redis). • CDN for audio: Host pre-generated question audio on CDN to reduce server bandwidth. 3110. Security & compliance Data Privacy • Voice recordings: Encrypted at rest, deleted after voice profile created (no long-term retention of raw audio). • Student responses: Stored in MongoDB with role-based access (only student + teacher + admin can view). • Proctoring frames: Deleted 30 days post-viva unless flagged for investigation. Authentication & Authorization • JWTtokens: Include userId, role, sessionId signed with secret. • Role-based routes: Teacher can only access vivas in their classrooms; student can only see their own reports. • Rate limiting: Limit API calls per user to prevent brute-force (e.g., 100 requests/minute). Academic Integrity • AI content detection: Eden AI flags suspiciously AI-generated responses. • Proctoring logs: Teachers review violations with visual evidence. • Transcription confidence: React Speech Recognition provides confidence scores; low confidence (<0.7) flagged for manual verification. GDPR & Data Retention • Data deletion: Viva reports deleted 1 year after course completion (conf igurable). • Export capability: Students can download all their viva data as JSON/PDF. • Audit logs: All system actions (viva started, evaluation completed, teacher reviewed proctoring) logged for compliance. 11. Conclusion The AI-powered viva system represents a paradigm shift in educational assessment—moving from resource-intensive, inconsistently-graded oral exams to scalable, rigorous, and equitable evaluations. By combining voice cloning, RAG-grounded question generation, real-time transcription, and vision-based proctoring, the system delivers authentic viva experiences at scale. The separation of AI processing into a Flask microservice ensures performance and f lexibility, while MongoDB’s flexible schema supports rapid iteration on data models. Teachers maintain control through question review and proctoring 32oversight, while students benefit from instant, personalized feedback grounded in curriculum. The full transcript and analysis enable both accountability and learning optimization.
